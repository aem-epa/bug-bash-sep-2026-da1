The tea families

From the brightest green to the deepest pu-erh, here's how the shelf breaks down.

Green

Steamed or pan-fired soon after picking to lock in a fresh, vivid character. The everyday hero of the shelf.

  • Origin China · Japan
  • Flavor Grassy, sweet, marine
  • Caffeine Low–Medium
  • Brew 80°C · 1–2 min

Matcha

Shade-grown green leaf stone-ground into a fine powder, so you drink the whole leaf whisked into water.

  • Origin Uji, Japan
  • Flavor Umami, creamy, vegetal
  • Caffeine High
  • Brew 75°C · whisk 15 sec

White

The least processed of all — young buds simply withered and dried. Delicate, subtle, and quietly complex.

  • Origin Fujian, China
  • Flavor Melon, hay, honey
  • Caffeine Low
  • Brew 80°C · 3–4 min

Oolong

Partly oxidized, the shape-shifter of tea — anywhere from light and floral to dark and roasted.

  • Origin Taiwan · Fujian
  • Flavor Floral, buttery, toasty
  • Caffeine Medium
  • Brew 90°C · 2–3 min

Black

Fully oxidized for a bold, malty cup that stands up to milk. The backbone of the breakfast pot.

  • Origin India · Sri Lanka
  • Flavor Malty, brisk, honeyed
  • Caffeine High
  • Brew 100°C · 3–4 min

Pu-erh

Aged and fermented, sometimes for years, into a smooth, earthy brew that mellows with every steeping.

  • Origin Yunnan, China
  • Flavor Earthy, woody, deep
  • Caffeine Medium–High
  • Brew 100°C · 3–5 min

Herbal

Not true tea at all, but infusions of flowers, roots and leaves. Naturally caffeine-free and endlessly varied.

  • Origin Worldwide
  • Flavor Floral, minty, fruity
  • Caffeine None
  • Brew 100°C · 5–7 min

It all starts with one plant

Green, white, oolong, black and pu-erh are all leaves of the same shrub — Camellia sinensis. What tells them apart is what happens after harvest: how much the leaf is withered, bruised, oxidized and fired.

Herbal blends are the exception. They're technically tisanes — infusions of other plants — with no true tea leaf, which is why they're naturally caffeine-free.
