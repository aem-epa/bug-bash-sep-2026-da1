Six reasons to keep the kettle on

Whole-leaf tea delivers more of the compounds that make tea worth drinking — here are the ones people notice most.

Rich in antioxidants

Green and white teas are full of catechins and polyphenols — the plant compounds behind tea's reputation as a feel-good drink.

Calm, steady focus

L-theanine, an amino acid found in tea, pairs with caffeine for alertness without the jitters or the crash.

Gentle energy

A cup has roughly a third to half the caffeine of coffee, released slowly — a lift you can feel but not fight.

Easy hydration

Unsweetened tea counts toward your daily fluids, so a habit that feels like a treat is quietly keeping you topped up.

Kind to digestion

Peppermint and ginger blends have soothed after-dinner stomachs for centuries — a warm mug is an old, good idea.

A better wind-down

Caffeine-free chamomile and rooibos signal the end of the day and make the evening feel a little more deliberate.

Brew it right

Great leaf is only half the cup. Get these four things close and the rest takes care of itself.

Heat the water

Delicate greens and whites like 75–85°C; blacks, oolongs and herbals want a full 95–100°C boil.

Measure the leaf

Start with one heaping teaspoon (about 2–3g) per cup, then adjust to your taste.

Steep by type

Green 1–2 min, oolong 2–3 min, black 3–4 min, herbal 5+ min. Taste as you go.

Re-steep it

Good whole leaf has more to give. Add 30 seconds to each of the next two or three infusions.
